0. RESEARCH QUESTION
----------------------------------------------------------------------
Does abnormal global English-language attention to a country, measured
by English Wikipedia page views, predict the relative returns of that
country's equity ETF over the following weeks?

"Relative" = relative to the other countries in the universe
(long high-attention countries, short low-attention countries).


1. HYPOTHESES
----------------------------------------------------------------------
H0 Efficient markets: attention is public and already in prices.
   -> Long-short return (LS) is zero at all horizons.

H1 Price pressure (Barber & Odean 2008; Da, Engelberg & Gao 2011):
   attention attracts uninformed buyers, pushing prices above fair
   value temporarily.
   -> LS positive over weeks 1-2, then reverses over weeks 3-12.

H2 Risk premium: attention spikes flag crises (war, election, default);
   investors demand compensation for the higher perceived risk, so
   prices drop and expected returns rise.
   -> LS positive and persistent, no reversal.

H1 and H2 can coexist; the horizon profile (section 7.4) shows which
dominates. The main test is two-sided: no sign is assumed in advance.


2. DATA
----------------------------------------------------------------------
2.1 Page views: Wikimedia REST API, per-article, daily.
    project = en.wikipedia, access = all-access, agent = user
    (excludes identified bots and spiders).
    Period: 2015-07-01 to 2026-09-30 (API starts 2015-07-01).
    Throttle to about 1 request/second, retry on HTTP 429.

2.2 Prices: Yahoo Finance via yfinance, daily adjusted close
    (dividends and splits included), 2014-07-01 to 2026-10-02.
    Daily volume for the liquidity filter.

2.3 Risk-free rate: Ken French Data Library, daily RF, compounded
    to weekly.

2.4 US dollar: UUP (Invesco DB US Dollar Index ETF), Yahoo, weekly
    return, used as a control factor.

Continuity check already done (2026-10-03): all candidate country
pages return 135 monthly observations 2015-07 to 2026-09 with no gap
and no rename break (minimum month >= 0.44 x median for every page).

4. SIGNAL
----------------------------------------------------------------------
4.1 Week w = Monday 00:00 to Sunday 24:00 UTC.
4.2 V(i,w) = sum of the 7 daily views of country i's page in week w.
4.3 Abnormal attention:
      ASV(i,w) = ln V(i,w) - ln median[ V(i,w-1), ..., V(i,w-8) ]
    Scale-free (big and small countries comparable) and robust to a
    one-off spike in the reference window.
4.4 A country needs all 9 weeks (w-8..w) of views to be ranked in
    week w; otherwise it is skipped that week.
4.5 Portfolios use only the cross-sectional rank of ASV (outliers such
    as Israel 2023 or Qatar World Cup 2022 cannot dominate).


5. TIMING
----------------------------------------------------------------------
5.1 Signal uses views up to Sunday 24:00 UTC of week w.
5.2 Rebalance at the adjusted close of the first US trading day of
    week w+1 (normally Monday; Tuesday after a US holiday).
5.3 Hold until the next rebalance close (one week later).
5.4 No look-ahead: all views used are dated before the trade.
    Wikimedia publishes a day's data some hours after midnight UTC,
    before the Monday US close; R6 (one-day delay) covers any lag.
5.5 Sample: first signal week = first week with 8 prior full weeks
    (Monday 2015-08-31 to Sunday 2015-09-06; first trade Tuesday
    2015-09-08, after Labor Day). Last signal week = 2026-09-14 to
    2026-09-20 (last full holding week ends Monday 2026-09-28).
5.6 If an ETF has no price after some date (e.g. ERUS 2022), the
    position is closed at the last available price and the ETF leaves
    the universe.


6. PORTFOLIOS
----------------------------------------------------------------------
6.1 Each week, sort the N eligible countries by ASV.
    High = top floor(N/3), Low = bottom floor(N/3), Middle = rest.
6.2 Equal weights inside each tercile.
6.3 LS(w+1) = mean return of High - mean return of Low
    (P&L per dollar on each side; market moves cancel out).


7. MAIN TESTS
----------------------------------------------------------------------
7.1 Mean weekly LS return, Newey-West t-statistic (4 lags), two-sided,
    5% level. H0: mean LS = 0.
7.2 Annualized Sharpe (mean / std x sqrt(52)), max drawdown of
    cumulative LS, hit rate (% of weeks with LS > 0).
7.3 Mean weekly return of High, Middle, Low separately (check that
    they are ordered).
7.4 Horizon profile: overlapping portfolios held h = 1, 2, ..., 12
    weeks (Jegadeesh-Titman: a new cohort each week, each cohort 1/h
    of capital). Plot cumulative LS by h.
    Reversal after weeks 1-2 -> H1; persistent -> H2.


8. IS IT NEW INFORMATION?
----------------------------------------------------------------------
8.1 Fama-MacBeth: each week, cross-sectional regression of next-week
    return on (all variables z-scored cross-sectionally that week):
      ASV rank
      R1   = return of the last week (to the rebalance close)
      R4   = return of weeks -4 to -2
      MOM  = return of weeks -52 to -5
      VOL  = std of daily returns over the last 26 weeks
      LVL  = ln median V(i, w-1..w-8)   (baseline attention level)
    Report time-series mean of each coefficient, Newey-West t (4 lags).

8.2 Alpha: time-series regression of LS on four factors built from
    the same universe, Newey-West t (4 lags):
      MKT = equal-weighted universe return - RF
      MOM = top tercile minus bottom tercile by MOM (weeks -52..-5)
      REV = bottom tercile minus top tercile by R1 (last week losers
            minus winners)
      USD = UUP weekly return
    Report alpha (weekly and annualized) and its t-statistic.


9. TRANSACTION COSTS
----------------------------------------------------------------------
9.1 One-way cost: 10 bps for Developed, 20 bps for Emerging ETFs.
9.2 Weekly cost = sum over both legs of |weight change| x cost of
    that ETF. Net LS = gross LS - cost.
9.3 Report mean weekly turnover, net mean LS, net Newey-West t, net
    Sharpe, and the break-even one-way cost
    (= gross mean LS / mean total turnover).


10. ROBUSTNESS (fixed list, no test added after seeing results)
----------------------------------------------------------------------
R1  Reference window of 4 or 12 weeks instead of 8.
R2  Quintiles instead of terciles; rank-weighted portfolio.
R3  "Economy_of_<country>" pages instead of country pages.
R4  Sub-periods: 2015-09 to 2019-12, 2020-01 to 2021-12,
    2022-01 to 2026-09.
R5  Leave one country out (re-run once per country).
R6  Trade one day later (second US trading day close).
R7  Placebo: shuffle ASV across countries within each week, 1,000
    times; empirical p-value = share of |placebo mean LS| >= |actual|.
R8  (if time) Global fear index: mean ASV of pages "Recession",
    "Stock_market_crash", "Inflation", "Bank_run"; compare LS in weeks
    of high vs low global fear (split at median).
R9  Exclude English-majority countries (US, UK, Canada, Australia,
    New Zealand, Ireland), where readers are largely domestic.


11. DECISION RULE: WOULD I TRADE IT WITH REAL CAPITAL?
----------------------------------------------------------------------
YES only if ALL hold:
  a) net-of-cost Newey-West |t| > 2;
  b) same sign in at least 2 of the 3 sub-periods (R4);
  c) alpha on the four factors (8.2) remains significant;
  d) break-even cost > 2 x assumed cost.
Otherwise NO, with the failing condition(s) stated.


12. EXECUTABLE STRATEGY (for a colleague, no questions needed)
----------------------------------------------------------------------
 1. Universe: the ETFs in section 3 that pass rules 3.2-3.4.
 2. Every Monday before the US close, download last week's (Mon-Sun
    UTC) daily English Wikipedia views (agent=user) for each country
    page in section 3, plus the 8 weeks before it.
 3. For each country: V = sum of last week's views;
    B = median of the 8 previous weekly sums; ASV = ln(V) - ln(B).
 4. Rank countries by ASV. Top third = High, bottom third = Low
    (round down; the middle is not traded).
 5. At the Monday close (Tuesday if Monday is a US holiday):
    go long High and short Low, equal dollar amounts in each ETF,
    equal total dollars on each side.
 6. Hold one week. At the next rebalance close, repeat steps 2-5 and
    trade only the differences.
 7. If an ETF stops trading, close it at its last price and drop it.
